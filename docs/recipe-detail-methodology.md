# Recipe detail methodology

Updated 2026-09-13. SnapChef's recipe detail layer turns each catalog entry into a practical four-serving cooking guide while preserving the short ingredient names used by pantry matching.

Each guide includes:

- measured ingredient starting points and preparation reminders;
- preparation time, cooking time, difficulty, and equipment;
- setup and cross-contamination reminders where raw protein is present;
- expanded heat, timing, texture, consistency, seasoning, finishing, serving, and storage steps; and
- protein-specific minimum-temperature or doneness cues.

The quantities are consistent home-kitchen starting points rather than claims of a single canonical version. Filipino, regional, and family recipes naturally vary, so every guide invites final adjustment for ingredient strength, preferred balance, and household style.

Food-safety cues follow the USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service minimum-temperature chart: poultry 74°C / 165°F, ground meats 71°C / 160°F, and fish and shellfish 63°C / 145°F. Whole cuts of beef, pork, veal, and lamb use 63°C / 145°F plus a three-minute rest. Source: <https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/safe-temperature-chart>

Recipe presentation conventions were cross-checked against established cooking references that separate servings, preparation and cooking times, measured ingredients, and ordered methods. Source example: <https://www.bbcgoodfood.com/recipes/tomato-sauce>

All expanded wording and measurement rules were written specifically for SnapChef. They do not reproduce source recipes. Users should use a clean food thermometer and account for appliance, cookware, altitude, ingredient-size, and regional-product differences.
